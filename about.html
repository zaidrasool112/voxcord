Who's behind this

About VOXCORD: two founders, one actual position on LinkedIn.

VOXCORD is a human-led LinkedIn ghostwriting agency run by two co-founders, Ahmad Mursal and Zaid Rasool. We are not a content agency that happens to work on LinkedIn. We exist because two people, working from different angles, kept noticing the same problem: the platform was starting to sound like one voice wearing thousands of names.

In plain terms

Who writes your LinkedIn posts?

A person writes every VOXCORD post, and that person is one of the two of us, Ahmad or Zaid. Both of us are involved in every lead and every client: we talk it through together, go through the work, give our opinions, and choose the stronger version before it reaches you. You review and approve your posts. AI tools may help with transcription, research, and early brainstorming, but they never write the sentences that get published.

This is also the direction LinkedIn itself is moving. Its chief product officer, Hari Srinivasan, has said LinkedIn should remain “a place where you can find real people and real perspectives,” and LinkedIn is replacing its AI “enhance your post” tool with a proofreading feature (Fortune).

Why VOXCORD exists

The pattern that started this

THE FIRST SIGN

Ahmad noticed it first. Across a run of copywriting projects, he kept seeing LinkedIn posts that were technically fine: clean grammar, correct structure, nothing a teacher would mark wrong. But they didn't sound like anyone in particular.

A DIFFERENT ANGLE

Zaid noticed it in a different way. Across his own copywriting work, he watched LinkedIn settle into a single rhythm. Post after post, from different founders in completely different industries, read like the same post wearing a different logo.

THEN AI SHOWED UP

AI writing tools were supposed to add range. Instead, a lot of AI-assisted content converged on the same handful of structures and the same handful of things to say. It made generic content easier to produce and harder to notice, and the sameness got louder, not quieter.

WHAT IT EXPOSED

It was never a volume problem, and never really about writing quality. It was a voice problem. “Professional” and “sounding like yourself” had quietly become treated as the same goal, and much ghostwriting, like most AI writing tools, was built to produce content fast, not to capture how a founder actually thinks.

THE DECISION

Build something that protects a founder's real voice against that flattening rhythm, instead of adding one more fast, generic operation to a platform that already has too many of them.

That problem hasn't gone away. AI just made generic content easier to produce and harder to notice as generic. That's still exactly what VOXCORD exists to work against.

LinkedIn has noticed too. In July 2026 it added a way for members to flag content that “Seems like AI slop” (TechCrunch), and in August its chief product officer, Hari Srinivasan, reported that views of content LinkedIn classifies as AI slop had dropped 40% within a few weeks (Fortune).

This is still what we build every post around.

What a LinkedIn ghostwriter needs

The skills behind every VOXCORD post

The defining skill of a LinkedIn ghostwriter is matching your voice, supported by interviewing, research, editing judgment, and discretion with confidential material. Here is how we hold ourselves to each.

INTERVIEWING

A weekly conversation of about 30 to 40 minutes, or an agreed alternative, where we ask the questions and you talk. You never arrive with a finished post.

VOICE CAPTURE

We work from your own words, stories, and preferred formats, and revise until a post sounds like you rather than like a template.

RESEARCH

A person researches every topic and checks the facts before a draft reaches you.

EDITING

Every draft is fully rewritten and edited by a human writer, and we cut anything generic.

DISCRETION

Anything you flag as confidential stays out of your posts by default. If something is unclear, we ask before publishing. How we handle your material.

RELIABILITY

A monthly content calendar and a steady publishing rhythm, so your profile stays consistent.

Worth being clear about

Our human-first standard

Every post has to pass the same test before it goes out. Our content is based on the founder's real experience, written in the founder's intended voice, researched and edited by people, and checked for accuracy.

Before any post goes out, we ask five questions

Is it based on something real?
Does it contain a specific insight?
Would the founder say this in a real conversation?
Does it respect confidentiality?
Would a relevant buyer find it useful?

If the answer to several of these is no, the post does not go out.

What we won't do

Present experiences you never had
Publish unsupported claims or manufactured controversy
Use your password or read your direct messages
Buy, automate, or fake engagement
Transparency

How we use AI

We use AI tools in a limited way: transcribing our conversations, researching, and brainstorming early angles or outlines. AI never generates the sentences that get published in your posts. Every draft is fully rewritten and edited by a human writer, and every final decision on ideas, voice, facts, and structure is human-led and approved by you.

This matters more than it used to. A post is not valuable because polished language was easy to produce. It needs a real observation, a specific experience, or a clear opinion that belongs to the founder, and that has to come from a person.

Your material

How we handle what you tell us

Interview recordings, notes, and business details are used only to deliver your service. We don't sell them or share them with third parties, except as needed to deliver the service or as the law requires. We record calls only with your signed consent.

Anything you mark as confidential is excluded from your posts by default. If it's unclear, we check with you before publishing rather than assuming it's safe to use.

We keep transcripts, drafts, and working notes for 30 days after a cancellation, in case you want to resume, and then delete them unless we agree otherwise in writing. You can ask for a copy of your data, or for earlier deletion, at any time. You own the final, approved content once you've paid for it.
